Reproducing models

Many scripts refer to the nodes, links and areas of a model by their ID, row number or geometry: the 01_fix_model.py scripts and model_edits.gpkg files of the gravity controlled models, and the feedback forms of the level controlled models. A model that is rebuilt from its source data must therefore get the same IDs, or all these references break. This page describes how the input models of the gravity controlled models were reproduced (#903), and the differences between library versions that were found on the way. They apply as well when the level controlled models are rebuilt.

The input models of the gravity controlled models

In July 2024, Sweco made the input models <authority>_2024_6_3 of the 11 gravity controlled water authorities with ribasim_lumping, at commit 65c7ef53 of #118, in four notebooks: apply manual changes to the HyDAMO data, lump the network into basins, and convert the result into a Ribasim model. This code is ported to ribasim_nl.lumping, and the DVC pipeline runs it:

  • pipeline/00a_process_hydamo.py applies the manual changes to the HyDAMO data of 7 water authorities. For Aa en Maas, De Dommel and Vallei en Veluwe, the processed HyDAMO data is source data.
  • pipeline/00b_lump_model.py lumps the HyDAMO network of 10 water authorities into their input model.
  • pipeline/noorderzijlvest/00_lump_model.py lumps the D-HYDRO model of Noorderzijlvest.

All node IDs and link IDs of the 11 input models are reproduced, and so are the row numbers (fids) of their basin areas, except in Limburg, whose input data differs from the data of the run in 2024. There, 3 basin areas are not assigned to a Basin, which shifts the fids of the others, so its fix script now selects basin areas by geometry, and 4 nodes get another name. The geometries of the basin areas differ slightly, see GEOS.

Process

  1. Find the code and the data as they were. The git history has the code, and the cloud storage has the data with its modification dates. Compare the dates of the inputs with those of the outputs: an input that changed after the run cannot reproduce it.
  2. Recreate the environment of the time, with the library versions of then, and run the original code on the current data. That gives the reference: differences between the reference and the original outputs come from the data, differences between the reference and the port come from the code.
  3. Port the code, and compare its intermediate results with those of the original code, table by table and including the order of the rows, not only the final model. The order of rows is often the order of the IDs.
  4. Where the order differs, emulate the old behaviour where the IDs follow from it, rather than sorting afterwards.
  5. Run the downstream scripts on the original and on the reproduced model, and compare their results.

For the input models, the reference environment was a separate Pixi project with Python 3.12, pandas 2.1.4, numpy 1.26.4, geopandas 0.14.4, shapely 2.0.4 with GEOS 3.12.2, fiona 1.9.6, pyogrio 0.7 and networkx 3.3. The environment Sweco used is not known, but this one reproduces its IDs.

GEOS

The results of geometric operations such as union, intersection, buffer(0) and make_valid differ between versions and builds of GEOS. Coordinates differ by about 1e-9 m, the results get other slivers and tiny holes, and adjacent parts of a union may or may not be merged. That cannot be emulated: GEOS 3.12.2 reproduces 858 of the 859 basin areas of De Dommel topologically, but only 190 of the 543 of Stichtse Rijnlanden.

The IDs do not depend on it, but scripts that select geometries do. They now allow for these differences:

  • Model.select_basin_area, used by remove_basin_area and update_basin_area, takes geometries as equal if their boundaries are on average at most 1 mm apart. Without a basin area that equals a polygon, it selects the basin areas that together make it up, or else splits the polygon from the basin area that contains it. So it works whether adjacent parts are merged or not.
  • ribasim_nl.geometry.connected_parts splits a (Multi)Polygon in the parts connected to the part with a given point, and the other parts, instead of taking the parts by position.

A union with slivers can also go wrong, in GEOS 3.12 as well as 3.14: it can lose or add whole parts. In Hunze en Aas, a union of 25 pieces of a basin area lost 2.1 km², and the union of a basin area of 61 vertices with a sliver of 1e-9 m² gained 0.066 km². ribasim_nl.geometry.union_polygons checks that a union is not larger than the polygons together, and contains a point inside each polygon. If not, it takes the union again with a fixed precision of 1 mm, or without the slivers. ribasim_nl.geodataframe.assign_node_ids_by_largest_overlap uses it. That check does not catch a part lost within a large polygon, though. Overlays with a fixed precision (grid_size), on geometries made valid at that precision with shapely.set_precision, are robust. ribasim_nl.berging.supply_area_basin_ids overlays basin areas with supply areas that way, since a floating point overlay found no overlap for 3 basins of Drents Overijsselse Delta that lie entirely in a supply area.

Some results remain sensitive to slivers. assign_node_ids_by_largest_overlap gives the holes in an area code to the basin area it overlaps most, also when that overlap is a sliver. An area code that lies in a hole may then go to another basin area, or to none, with the regenerated input model: this changes 5 basin areas of Rijn en IJssel by up to 0.6 km².

Writing reproducible code

  • Sort with kind="stable", and with keys that decide the order of all rows that matter. The order of equal values in an unstable sort depends on the library version and the CPU.
  • Do not rely on the order of rows after a merge, a groupby or a spatial join; sort explicitly where the order sets IDs.
  • Refer to objects by a stable key, such as their code, rather than by row number. gpd.read_file(..., fid_as_index=True) makes a script depend on the order of the rows in the file, so keep that order when such a file is rewritten.
  • Select geometries with a tolerance, and do not rely on the order or number of the parts of geometries from geometric operations.
  • Where the result of an overlay decides something, overlay with a fixed precision, on geometries made valid at that precision.
  • The DVC pipeline records the code, data and environment of every output, in git, dvc.lock and pixi.lock, so an output can always be reproduced.

Moving fixes into the lumping

The 01_fix_model.py scripts correct the input models afterwards. Now that the pipeline makes the input models, most of these fixes can move into the lumping or its inputs, or the lumping algorithm can change, so that the input models are right from the start:

  • Holes in the basin areas. ribasim_nl.lumping.network.assign_unassigned_areas gives areas without a basin to the neighbouring basin area they share most with, but only within their drainage area, and not for Vallei en Veluwe. The fix scripts of 6 authorities fill the remaining holes from verwerkt/4_ribasim/areas.gpkg: Brabantse Delta, Hunze en Aas, Rijn en IJssel and Vechtstromen with assign_node_ids_by_largest_overlap, Aa en Maas and De Dommel with code of their own. They overlay the areas with the basin areas again, so slivers can decide where an area goes. The lumping knows the basin of each area directly. Choose a rule there for areas without a neighbour in their drainage area, for instance the neighbour with the longest shared boundary, and keep exclusions, such as the Twentekanaal areas of Vechtstromen, as source data. Then the hole filling, fix_unassigned_basin_area and remove_unassigned_basin_area can go from the fix scripts.
  • One areas file. For Brabantse Delta, De Dommel and Hunze en Aas, verwerkt/4_ribasim/areas.gpkg differs from the verwerkt/3_input/areas.gpkg of the lumping.
  • Edits of the lumping inputs. Splitting basin areas with lines, or removing and renaming them with model_edits.gpkg, can become edits of the split nodes, boundaries and areas that the lumping reads, which have stable codes. The tolerant Model.select_basin_area only bridges until then.
  • Node IDs. They follow from the order of the boundaries, split nodes and basins, so a new split node renumbers all basins after it. That breaks the references to node IDs in the remaining fix scripts, in model_edits.gpkg, and in pipeline/09_koppelen_modellen.py. Move the fixes of an authority together, and where references remain, refer to nodes by a stable key, such as their code.
